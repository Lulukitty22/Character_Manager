## Transformations are separate from subclass

The historical Discord plan lists Tasha's Otherworldly Guise at **Wizard11**. Sixth-tier spells unlock at Wizard11; Wizard12 is sufficient. Guise is a spell, not Bladesinger14. Any eligible Wizard specialty can use it under the campaign's acquisition policy. Song of Victory at Wizard14 is a different weapon feature; the2025 published version follows spells with a weapon strike, not flight.

Legacy spell reference summary, **full published text still to verify before implementation**: Tasha's Otherworldly Guise is a flying combat form with+2AC, spellcasting-ability weapon use, two attacks that do not stack with Extra Attack, selected immunities and one-minute concentration. Tenser's Transformation is a different sixth-tier weapon-combat spell, with temporary HP/extra weapon damage and a spellcasting prohibition, not flight. Do not conflate them or grant both effects under one name. Official spell routes were unavailable/redirected to store in this check; no new transformation mechanics approved from secondary sites.

The old Faust robe explicitly combines Guise and Bladesong, with up to **one minute to activate**. One minute is10rounds, longer than the user's typical fight. This activation is an open custom-item problem, not the ordinary spell/Bladesong action cost. Never restore retired memory costs. Do not silently preserve an in-combat10-round windup when drafting the rebuilt item.

## Defense and AC

At Wizard12/INT22, Abjurer's ward max=2×12+6=**30**. It protects the Wizard's HP; it is not+30AC or permanent+30maxHP. An ally can receive protection through Projected Ward using a Reaction and the **same** ward pool. No obligation to spend it on others. Casting eligible Abjuration spells with slots recharges the ward; current version also permits slot-funded bonus-action recharge. Exact final action/trigger text still to encode.

Illustrative AC assumes historical DEX16 and no extra magic-item bonuses. Bladesong numbers below use **verified Tasha-era mechanics**, pending full published2025 text, not UA as final.

| Setup | AC | With reaction Shield spell |
|---|---|---|
| Mage Armor:13+DEX3 |16|21 |
| Mage Armor + Bladesong INT6 |22|27 |
| Half plate + physical shield, if trained and compatible with hands/items |19|24 |

Abjurer/Evoker have no automatic subclass AC bonus here. Bladesong cannot combine with a physical shield or incompatible armor/weapon handling. Its Shield **spell** is different from using a physical shield. Tasha-era Song of Defense can spend a Reaction/slot to reduce one damage instance by5×slot tier (third slot15;fifth25); this competes with Shield/Counterspell for that Reaction. Bladesong does not require concentration, whereas Guise does. With the legacy Guise+2AC summary, Mage Armor/Guise would be18 and adding compatible Bladesong24; these Guise combinations remain provisional until full spell/version verification and item decisions.

Against illustrative enemy attack+9: AC16 is hit70%, AC22 is hit40%; with Shield, AC21 is hit45%, AC27 hit15%. Normal attacks only, no advantage/disadvantage. High AC does not prevent saving-throw damage or critical hits. Ward absorbs damage rather than changing hit chance; different defenses solve different problems.

## HP and action economy

At assumed CON14 and fixed-average level HP, Wizard12/Artificer3 would have **95HP Wizard-start or96 Artificer-start**, before extra grants. Calculation Wizard-start:6+11×4+3×5+15×2=95. Actual scores, start class and campaign roll policy remain open. The old sheet's low maxHP is not a reliable prediction of this rebuilt total15 character. A full ward would add30 initial absorption, not turn95maxHP into125maxHP.

Six rounds leave only six main actions. Unspent slots are not automatically a problem: evaluate output per turn, concentration, survival and number of encounters before rest. Shield, ward recharge and Song of Defense can use otherwise unspent slots for survival. Guise and Bladesong both use bonus actions in standard reference rules, so cannot both start in the same turn; Guise's slot-funded casting also interacts with2024's one-slot-cast-per-turn rule. No free precombat setup assumed in the damage table.

Decision pointer: Abjurer is viable for a primary damage dealer whose bottleneck is surviving; it keeps core damage spells and Guise access. Bladesinger offers higher conditional AC and weapon play. Evoker gives measurable direct-spell damage/safer area casting, but loses those specific subclass defenses. More party DPS depends on remaining conscious as well as damage dice; do not declare one universally best without actual enemy/target counts and Carol's preferred actions.
